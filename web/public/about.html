About

who the atlas is for, and what each reader can do with it

The Symbolic World helps analysts, students, readers of alchemy and scholars of Jung find what Jung wrote and check it. Each connection between concepts carries a verbatim quote from the Collected Works, with its volume and paragraph, so any reader can test a claim against the book.

Analysts and trainees can look up where Jung treats a concept and follow it across volumes, with a citation ready to carry into their own work. The query page filters by volume, family and claim type, and it searches the words inside the quotes.

Students can start from the ranked concepts index to see which ideas Jung returns to most, then open any concept's page for everything the graph holds on it. The routes page traces how one concept reaches another.

Readers of alchemy can use the concordance, which sets Jung's psychological concepts beside the alchemical figures and operations he reads them through, with the citation for each pairing.

Scholars can treat the atlas as an inspectable index to the Collected Works. Every link names its source paragraph, says whether Jung asserts the claim, reports a doctrine or quotes a source, and records who checked it. Disputes sit on the public log. The original structure and metadata are open for reuse under CC BY 4.0, and the quotations stay with their rights holders.

The atlas maps what Jung says. A machine checks every quotation against the text. A documented procedure assigns the relation families, and a person can rule where the procedure disagrees. Neither step replaces scholarly review. The method page sets out what the checks cover and what they leave out.
