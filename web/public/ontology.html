the ontology

What this graph is, as an ontology

It is a network of asserted relations between named things, each relation carrying the paragraph it came from and a quote from it. It is not a subsumption ontology, and most of what an ontology engineer would look for is missing on purpose. This page says what the design is, what it leaves out and why, and then runs the standard pitfall scan over the graph and reports what it finds — including the findings that are these decisions showing up as defects.

Two tiers

The first tier is the relation as Jung's sentence phrased it. It is frozen: the string is part of the claim that was gated, and a model of a different family judged the paragraph against exactly that wording. Rewriting it into a controlled predicate would change what was verified.

The second tier is the family — a classification over the verified relation, drawn from a fixed vocabulary in a versioned document, assigned when two models reading that document independently name the same family, and ruled by a person when they do not. The second tier never reopens the first. Nothing on this site calls a family verified, and no family assignment changes a relation string.

Where a relation carried a whole clause, a normalization layer records a cleaned verb phrase beside the frozen original, with the qualifiers pulled out into their own fields. 260 relations carry that layer. The family layer classifies the normalized form where it exists, which is why the map counts effective strings rather than surface ones.

The declarations

Two properties are declared, and only two: which relation strings are inverses of each other, and which are symmetric. Each declaration is transcribed from a line of the definitions document, cites that line, and is applied only when a reader asks for closure — the derived row is labelled, carries its source edge's references, and is never written into the graph.

Deliberately absent

  • Transitivity. No family is transitive, and the identity walk says so above its first hop. Two terms joined through a third are joined through a third; the graph does not claim they are the same.
  • Domain and range as constraints. The signatures a relation is observed with are a measurement of the corpus. As a constraint they would reject an edge for disagreeing with the schema rather than with the paragraph.
  • Disjoint types. A node can work in two registers at once, and a disjointness axiom would force a choice the material does not make.
  • A subsumption backbone. There is no hierarchy to inherit through, because none was claimed. The type tag is a coarse label, not a class system.
  • A closed relation vocabulary. The predicate is Jung's phrasing. Closing the set would mean editing claims, so the set stays open and the family layer carries the classification.

P10 missing disjointness axioms
The 6 node types are never declared disjoint, and nothing forbids a node from being conceptually two at once (e.g. Mercurius is modelled Figure but functions as Substance and Symbol across its 52 edges). Type is a single free-text field.

A stance is not a rebuttal. Every design decision here has a cost, and the scan is a fair statement of it: an open vocabulary does make the graph hard to query by predicate, which is exactly why the family layer exists and why its coverage is published with its gaps. What the panel refuses is the other reading — that a decision taken deliberately, with a reason on record, is a defect discovered by a tool.
