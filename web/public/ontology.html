Evidence

The design

Why the graph has two tiers, what its quality scan found, and how it compared with outside scholarship.

The atlas is a network of asserted relations between named things. Each relation rests on a paragraph of the Collected Works and a quote from it. This page records the design decisions and the results of a standard pitfall scan over the graph.

The terms the RDF export uses for claims, references and their checks are on the vocabulary page.

Two tiers

Jung's relations come in many wordings, so the graph has two tiers.

The first tier is the relation in Jung's own wording.

The second tier is the family: a classification over the verified relation. Two models read the family definitions and classify each relation without seeing the other's answer. Where they name the same family, the assignment stands. Where they differ, a person rules. The families page sets out the whole procedure.

The families are this project's sorting of the relations Jung wrote. The field has not agreed on them and the graph does not claim they are his structure.

Where a relation was a whole clause, a normalization layer records a cleaned verb phrase beside the frozen original. 260 relations carry that layer. The family layer classifies the normalized form where it exists.

The pitfall catalogue

The scan follows the OOPS! catalogue, the OntOlogy Pitfall Scanner, which numbers the modelling faults that recur in published ontologies and describes how to detect each one. The catalogue is a list of known issues rather than a standard to comply with and so can provide design guidance. Our scan script implements a subset against this graph and keeps the catalogue's numbering so a finding can be looked up in the literature. It is not the OOPS! service and does not run its full test set.

Poveda-Villalón, Gómez-Pérez and Suárez-Figueroa, "OOPS! (OntOlogy Pitfall Scanner!): An On-line Tool for Ontology Evaluation", International Journal on Semantic Web and Information Systems 10(2), 2014.

critical
P22 no naming/vocabulary regularity
1132/1630 relation strings (69%) are used exactly once. The relation set is an open vocabulary.
 recorded design decision
The relation slot holds Jung’s own predication, frozen verbatim. A closed property set would mean editing the claim to fit the schema, so the vocabulary stays open and the family layer classifies over it. An open vocabulary is hard to query by predicate, which is why the family layer and the routes exist.
critical
P24 relation name encodes a proposition
270 relation strings are sentence-shaped: the predicate carries content that belongs in the object or a qualifier, so it cannot be queried as written.
 open: recorded as a defect
A predicate carrying a whole proposition is the project’s named defect. New mining is constrained by a form rule, and the normalization layer repairs the old edges beside their frozen originals rather than over them.
critical
P02/P30 synonymous relations, 6 groups
  • identity/equivalence: 12 distinct relation strings over 165 edges
  • symbolisation: 13 distinct relation strings over 478 edges
  • analogy/correspondence: 10 distinct relation strings over 277 edges
  • part/containment: 10 distinct relation strings over 131 edges
  • generation/origin: 15 distinct relation strings over 152 edges
  • transformation: 8 distinct relation strings over 45 edges
In each group no equivalence is declared, so a query on any one string misses the rest.
 answered by the family layer
A query by family returns every assigned relation in that family, whatever its string. Relations still waiting for a ruling are not returned. No string is declared equivalent to another, because each edge is assigned in its own paragraph.
critical
P06/no backbone: taxonomy is vestigial
Only 94/4829 edges (1.9%) are taxonomic and only 77/1156 nodes (7%) have any parent. There is no subsumption hierarchy to inherit or reason over. The graph is an associative network with a 6-way type tag.
 recorded design decision
By design. The paper describes the graph as an associative network with a type tag.
important
P05/P13 inverse relations declared but not linked
9 inverse pairs coexist in the vocabulary (symbolizes/symbolized-by, amplified-by/amplifies, precedes/followed-by, contains/belongs-to, produces/produced-by, mother-of/child-of, father-of/child-of, is-goal-of/aims-at, exemplified-by/exemplifies). Nothing declares them inverse, so direction is a modelling choice made per-edge; 3 pairs are asserted in both directions and would be duplicates under a reasoner.
 answered by the declarations layer
The declarations name the inverse pairs the document rules on. They are applied when a query asks for closure and are never written into the graph.
important
P25 symmetric relation asserted one-way only
393 edges use a symmetric relation but only assert one direction. Without a symmetry axiom, `A synonym-of B` does not answer a query for B's synonyms.
 answered by the declarations layer
Same answer: a symmetric declaration is read at query time. Asserting the second direction in the graph would put a claim in it that no paragraph was gated for.
important
P30 identity clusters not collapsed / no transitivity
3 clusters of 3+ nodes are chained by identity-type relations without a transitivity axiom or a canonical representative. These are candidate duplicate entities.
 recorded design decision
Transitivity is rejected on every family. Identity chains can be read one edge at a time by the identity walk in the query tool, which prints each hop’s own citation and asserts nothing about the ends; it is not exposed on the site in this release.
important
P04 unconnected ontology element
5 components detached from the main graph.
 open: recorded as a defect
A detached component is a gap in what has been mined, not a decision.
important
P24 node label is a definition, not a term
41 nodes carry a full descriptive sentence as their label. These are assertions modelled as entities, so they cannot be reused or matched across edges.
 open: recorded as a defect
A label that is a sentence cannot be matched across edges. Counted here, not yet repaired.
important
P11/P12 no domain or range declared
No relation has a declared domain/range. Observed signatures are unconstrained: `corresponds-to` spans 28 distinct (subject-type, object-type) pairs across 164 edges. Nothing can detect a mis-typed edge.
 recorded design decision
No relation is given a domain or a range as a constraint. The observed signatures are a measurement of the corpus; a constraint would reject an edge because of what the schema expects rather than what the paragraph says.
important
P02 ambiguous predicate `is`
`is` is used 61 times, conflating subsumption, identity and predication. Cannot be interpreted without reading the quote.
 recorded design decision
`is` is the word Jung used. The family assignment disambiguates it per edge, in the paragraph it came from, and the verbatim string stays as written.
minor
P21 catch-all class
`Concept` holds 467/1156 nodes (40%) with no subdivision. The type system carries little discriminating power.
 open: recorded as a defect
The type tag is coarse and does little work. It colours the map and picks the Concordance registers; nothing rests on it.
minor
P10 missing disjointness axioms
The 6 node types are never declared disjoint, and nothing forbids a node from being conceptually two at once (e.g. Mercurius is modelled Figure but functions as Substance and Symbol across its 52 edges). Type is a single free-text field.
 recorded design decision
Types are not declared disjoint. A node can function in two registers at once, and the graph would have to choose one to satisfy a disjointness axiom.

Checked against readers of Jung

In August 2026 we put a sample of these assignments to the secondary literature: 27 items, drawn by seeded stratification and weighted toward the contested families, checked in three pre-registered passes against eight works of Jungian scholarship: Jacobi's The Psychology of C. G. Jung, Samuels, Shorter and Plaut's A Critical Dictionary of Jungian Analysis, von Franz's Alchemy: An Introduction to the Symbolism and the Psychology, four volumes of Edinger, and Abraham's A Dictionary of Alchemical Imagery. The check asked whether the kind of relation we recorded is the kind a reader of the literature would say Jung asserts.

The literature treats 16 of the 22 sampled assignments (the other 5 of the 27 items were queued arm-disagreements, scored separately); scholars' renderings agreed with our family on 8 of those 16, disagreed on 6, and split among themselves on 2. The other 6 pairs were not treated at all. The literature often does not discuss Jung's assertions about single images. The disagreements produced seven rule changes, folded into the ruleset as v0.7; the largest sends every amplified-by edge back for reclassification, because two independent reference authors read those relations as content claims, not adduced parallels.
