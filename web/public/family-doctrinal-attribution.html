Definition

Quoted from docs/FAMILY_DEFINITIONS.md v0.7.2, family 21. The atlas quotes that document; it never paraphrases it.

A carrier names, defines, translates, teaches, interprets or classifies — the relation records an act of predication by someone, not an ontology between subject and object.

Boundary: the decisive test — remove the carrier and the claim collapses. Voice often co-varies but never decides (rule 3). Tradition-to-tradition transmission is derivation; a single carrier's predication is attribution.

A carrier is "named" by definite reference, not only by proper name: (v0.7, ruling 5): "this remarkable text explains…" identifies a carrier; collective ascription ("the alchemists say") stays with the content family unless the claim collapses without the ascription. Genre note, from the external check: dictionary-genre sources state relations carrier-free, so dictionary evidence systematically under-reports attribution — weigh attribution questions toward discursive sources.

Attribution outranks the surface verb.: When a NAMED carrier performs the predication, the family is attribution no matter what the relation string says — a symbolizes/symbolized-by/parallels string does not send the edge to symbolization or correspondence if the quote's claim is the carrier's act of reading. Worked example: the-opus symbolized-by peregrinatio (14 §199, "Maier imagined the opus as a wandering") is doctrinal-attribution, not symbolization — Maier's act; remove Maier and this quote's claim collapses. Contrast a symbolization edge whose quote asserts the image relation with no carrier named in the quote itself.
