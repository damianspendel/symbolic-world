How the atlas is made

One claim,
checked three ways.

The Symbolic World is a map of C. G. Jung’s ideas. Every line on it is a claim — this concept relates to that one — resting on an exact quote from his Collected Works. This page follows one claim through the machine that decides what gets in.

IThe map

Each dot is a concept. Each line says how two concepts are related, in words backed by a quoted sentence from the books.

The atlas opens on the lapis — the philosophers’ stone — so we start there too.

Follow the red line.

IIThe machine
1
Proposed — Claude, Opus family

An AI reads one paragraph at a time and proposes claims, each with the exact sentence it rests on.

lapis is projection of the self
“we would say that the stone is a projection of the unified self”
Collected Works vol. 9, part II · ¶ 264
2
Checked — Claude, Fable family

A second AI, from a different model family, re-reads the paragraph and judges every claim against it. The model that proposes is never the model that approves.

3
Audited — Google Gemini

A third AI, from a different company, checks the whole batch again, independently. Our claim came back supported.

4
Refereed — a human

When the checkers disagree, a person reads the paragraph and rules. Nothing on this line marks its own homework.

IIIThe receipt

Every claim that survives is published with everything needed to check it yourself: the book, the paragraph, the quote.
