## Findings — final state

**F2 revised (the sentence-subject test): present in the literature's usage,
absent from its concepts.** The strongest single result of v3. Edinger's own
predications fall exactly along the v0.6 line — copula to the term-pair
("The Self … is identical with the imago Dei", E&A p. 3), symbol language to
the image ("expressed by certain typical symbolic images called mandalas",
p. 4) — twenty lines apart, without his ever remarking the subject changed.
The Critical Dictionary does the same across three entries. So the test
formalizes a regularity scholars follow without theorizing it. Weaker than
external endorsement, far stronger than v1's "no counterpart": the rule
tracks real usage; no scholar recognizes it as a distinction.

**F11 (new): the charter-named pair is not a pair the literature forms.**
Neither new book ever puts mandala and God-image in one predication: both
route mandala → Self, and the Self carries the identity with the God-image.
The graph asserts a pair the sources decline to form — which supports §60's
correspondence-of-symbols reading and leaves §572's identity ruling resting
on the paragraph alone.

**F3 closed: idiom instability is the norm, in both directions.** Abraham,
Edinger-across-books, Edinger-within-one-book (E&A p. 132 equates
Christ/Anthropos; Mysterium renders it doctrine+correspondence; E&A p. 267
un-equates Stone/Christ). Secondary-literature idiom cannot anchor identity
assignments; only the primary text's own predication can.

F1 (amplification 0/3, two-source), F5 (Jacobi backs position), F6
(dictionary genre flattens carriers), F9 (named-figure is-a rule — now a
SPLIT rather than clean counter-evidence, softening P7's urgency but not its
direction), F10 (identity↔correspondence queue shape is a genuine scholarly
fault line) all stand as stated in v1/v2.
